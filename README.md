# ICSR-Case-Processing-


What is an ICSR?

An Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) is a report containing information about a suspected adverse drug reaction or other safety-related event in an individual patient who has used a medicinal product.

Example: A patient develops a skin rash after taking an antibiotic. This information may be reported and processed as an ICSR.

 Sources of ICSR

ICSRs are received from two main sources:

A. Solicited Sources

These are reports collected through organized methods such as clinical trials, patient support programmes, and post-marketing studies.

B. Unsolicited Sources

These are reports received voluntarily from healthcare professionals, patients, consumers, or other sources without a specific request to collect the report.

What is a Safety Database?

A safety database is an electronic system used to collect, store, process, review, and track safety reports related to medicinal products.

Examples: Oracle Argus Safety, Veeva Safety, and ArisGlobal LifeSphere Safety.

Day Zero and Regulatory Timelines

Day Zero is the date on which the company or its authorized representative first receives the minimum information required for a valid ICSR.

Regulatory timelines are the deadlines for submitting safety reports to the appropriate regulatory authority.

Regulatory Reporting Timelines in India

- Death or life-threatening cases: Within 7 calendar days.
- Other serious cases: Within 15 calendar days.
- Non-serious cases: Within 30 calendar days.
- Medical device malfunction cases: Within 90 calendar days, where applicable under the relevant reporting requirements.


5. ICSR Case Processing Steps

Step 1: Validity Check

The processor checks whether the report contains the four minimum criteria:

- An identifiable patient
- An identifiable reporter
- A suspected medicinal product
- A suspected adverse event or reaction

If any required information is missing, follow-up may be needed to obtain a valid case.

Step 2: Duplicate Check

The processor checks whether the same case has already been received and entered into the safety database. This helps prevent duplicate case records.

Step 3: Triage

Triage means checking and classifying incoming reports based on factors such as seriousness, source, report type, and processing priority.

Step 4: Query for Missing Information and Follow-up

If important information is missing, a query is sent to the reporter to obtain additional details.

Examples include the patient's medical history, drug dosage, treatment dates, laboratory results, and outcome of the event.

Step 5: Data Entry and Medical Coding

The available information is entered into the safety database.

Medical terms are coded using standardized dictionaries:

- MedDRA: Used to code adverse events, diagnoses, symptoms, and medical conditions.

- WHO Drug Dictionary (WHO-DD): Used to identify and code medicinal products.

- Company Dictionary: May be used for company-specific terminology or additional internal classification, according to company procedures.

Step 6: Narrative Writing

A case narrative is a short, clear summary of the patient's case. It includes relevant information about the patient, suspected drug, adverse event, treatment, medical history, and outcome.

Step 7: Quality Review of ICSR

A quality reviewer checks whether the case has been processed correctly and according to company procedures.

The review may include checking:

- Patient and reporter information
- Drug details and medical coding
- Event dates and treatment dates
- Narrative accuracy and completeness
- Seriousness and causality information
- Regulatory reporting requirements

If any errors or missing information are identified, the reviewer may raise a query or return the case for correction.

Step 8: Medical Review of ICSR

A qualified medical reviewer evaluates the clinical information in the case.

The review may include:

A. Causality Assessment: Evaluating whether the suspected drug may have caused the reported event.

B. Seriousness Assessment: Determining whether the event meets any regulatory seriousness criterion, such as death, life-threatening condition, hospitalization, disability, congenital anomaly, or another medically important condition.

C. Medical Coding Review: Checking whether the selected medical terms accurately represent the reported symptoms, diagnoses, and events.

D. Company Comment: Recording the company's assessment or position regarding the case, when required by applicable procedures.

E. Medical Sense Check: Checking whether the information is clinically understandable, consistent, and medically reasonable.

Step 9: Final Case Correction and Approval

Any issues identified during quality or medical review are corrected. Additional queries may be sent if clarification is needed. The case is finalized according to the company's review and approval procedures.

Step 10: ICSR Submission

Before submission, the case is checked for completeness, validity, correct coding, required approvals, and applicable reporting timelines.

The finalized ICSR is submitted electronically or through the required process to the appropriate regulatory authority, as applicable.

Submission details and confirmation are recorded in the safety database.

6. Qualifications and Experience Required

Requirements vary by company, country, and job role.

A. Triage, Data Entry, Quality Review, and Submission

Common eligible backgrounds may include:

- B.Pharm or M.Pharm
- Pharm.D
- Nursing
- Other life science or healthcare-related degrees accepted by the employer

Some positions require prior pharmacovigilance experience, often around one year or more. Freshers may be eligible for trainee, internship, or entry-level data processing positions.

B. Medical Review

Medical review positions generally require an MBBS or another medical qualification specified by the employer. Some senior medical review positions require an MD or relevant clinical experience.

7. Conclusion

ICSR case processing is an important activity in pharmacovigilance. It involves receiving safety reports, checking validity, identifying duplicates, entering data, coding medical terms, writing narratives, reviewing cases, assessing medical information, and submitting reports within the applicable regulatory timelines. These activities help monitor the safety of medicines and protect patients.
